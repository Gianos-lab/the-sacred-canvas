There's something almost compulsive about worldbuilding. You start with a single question—what if magic worked like this?—and suddenly you're designing pantheons, mapping continents, inventing languages. Before you know it, you have notebooks full of cosmology and no actual story to put it in.

I've been doing this privately for years. Building worlds in isolation, keeping the notes to myself, waiting for some hypothetical future where everything would be "finished" enough to share. But that future never arrives. There's always another detail to refine, another inconsistency to resolve.

## A Different Approach

This project is an experiment in doing the opposite: building in public from day one.

Instead of waiting until the world is complete, I'm documenting the process as it unfolds. The false starts. The contradictions. The moments where I realize I've accidentally created a massive plot hole in my own cosmology.

The goal isn't perfection—it's coherence. I want to create a fictional universe that feels lived-in and real, where the pieces fit together because they were developed together, not bolted on afterward.

## What This Looks Like

Concretely, this means developing several elements in parallel:

- **Cosmology**: How did this universe begin? What forces shape it?
- **History**: What major events have occurred? Who were the key actors?
- **Language**: What tongues do people speak? How does language reflect culture?
- **Culture**: What do societies value? How do they organize themselves?

These aren't separate projects. They're threads in the same tapestry, each informing the others. A creation myth shapes religious practice. Language structure reflects cosmological understanding. Historical events echo the logic of the universe itself.

## Why Public?

Honestly? Accountability.

Private worldbuilding can spiral forever. There's no forcing function, no reason to make decisions and move forward. Publishing forces clarity. It means committing to ideas, even if they're provisional. It means explaining choices, which makes you think harder about whether they actually make sense.

Plus, there's something valuable about showing the work. Most worldbuilding tutorials present the finished product—here's a fully realized culture, complete with customs and calendar. But they rarely show the messy process of getting there. The dead ends. The revisions. The moments of "wait, that doesn't work at all."

This blog is that process made visible.

## What's Next

The immediate work is establishing the foundational cosmology—the origin story of this universe and the metaphysical rules that govern it. From there, language and history will develop in parallel, each providing constraints and inspiration for the other.

I have no idea how long this will take. Months? Years? It doesn't really matter. The point isn't to finish quickly. It's to build something coherent, interesting, and alive.

Welcome to the workshop.
